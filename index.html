Curriculum overview Remembrance Day art installation, Toddler to Grade 8

A Field of Remembrance is a collaborative, school-wide art installation designed to transform the front lawn into a symbolic field of 1,000 red poppies. Inspired by the imagery of In Flanders Fields and the orderly rows of military graves, the installation brings together mathematics, visual arts, geometry, measurement, practical life, Canadian history, and Montessori peace education.

Each student contributes to the installation by preparing ten templated poppies using red metallic paper. The reflective surface captures natural sunlight, creating a vibrant field of red that changes in appearance throughout the day. Each poppy is mounted on an 18-inch dowel painted black, secured with a two-inch piece of black duct tape, and finished by painting the white reverse side of the metallic paper.

The installation is designed not only as an artistic tribute but also as a concrete mathematical experience. For CASA and Primary Years students, the opportunity to see, count, organize, and physically construct a collection of 1,000 objects provides a powerful representation of quantity and the decimal system.

Students in Grades 1–3 assume responsibility for constructing a grid across the front lawn using caution tape. The repeated printed word CAUTION becomes a visual measuring unit, allowing children to identify consistent intervals and position their poppies independently. Through this process, students discover how equal spacing, alignment, repetition, and geometric organization transform individual objects into a unified installation.

The completed field serves as a visual expression of remembrance, demonstrating how individual contributions become part of a collective tribute to those who served and sacrificed.

Educational intentions

Mathematics

Geometry and measurement

Visual arts

Repetition, colour and light

The installation investigates shape, colour, reflective surfaces, contrast, rhythm, repetition, and the visual impact of arranging many identical forms into one large composition.

Montessori practical life

History and peace education

Collective remembrance

The poppy symbolizes remembrance, while the ordered field introduces the historical imagery of Flanders and encourages age-appropriate reflection on sacrifice, gratitude, and peace.

Mathematics The concrete representation of 1,000

The central mathematical intention of A Field of Remembrance is to provide children with an opportunity to experience the magnitude of 1,000 through a tangible, three-dimensional representation.

For Grades 1–3, the installation extends into skip-counting, repeated addition, multiplication, arrays, and the organization of quantities into rows and columns.

Geometry and measurement The caution tape grid

The Primary Years students’ responsibility for constructing the caution tape grid introduces an authentic mathematical problem: how can we position hundreds of poppies so that every row is straight and the distance between each poppy is consistent?

Rather than relying on adults to measure and mark every position, students use the repeated printed word CAUTION as a visual reference for spacing.

This approach supports:

  • Non-standard units of measurement and repeated intervals.
  • Recognition of parallel and perpendicular lines.
  • Understanding rows, columns, and arrays.
  • Visual estimation, alignment, and correction of error.
  • Independence through a prepared environment that allows children to position their own work.

For mathematical accuracy, students should use a consistent reference point on each printed word, such as the beginning of the letter C. The actual printed repeat distance should be checked before laying the grid, since caution tape does not necessarily provide standardized measurement intervals.

Visual arts Material, light and composition

The choice of red metallic paper is integral to the artistic intention of the installation.

Unlike ordinary construction paper, the reflective surface interacts with natural sunlight, creating changing highlights and intensifying the visual presence of the red poppies across the lawn.

Students investigate the relationship between material and light, while the contrast between the red petals and black stems creates a strong, unified visual language.

The repetition of a single templated form allows students to explore how individual shapes become part of a much larger composition. The visual rhythm of the carefully aligned rows creates a sense of order, while the sheer quantity of poppies communicates the scale of collective remembrance.

The finished installation is both an artwork and an environment through which students can observe how repetition, spacing, colour, and light contribute to meaning.

Practical life Preparing the poppies

The production process incorporates a purposeful sequence of work:

  1. Cutting. Students cut ten poppy shapes from red metallic paper using prepared templates, refining hand-eye coordination and scissor control.
  2. Painting the stems. Students paint the 18-inch wooden dowels black, practising controlled movement, brush handling, and care of materials.
  3. Attaching. Students secure the dowel to the reverse of each poppy using a two-inch piece of black duct tape, developing coordination and sequencing.
  4. Finishing. Students paint the white reverse side of the paper, attending to completeness and visual consistency.
  5. Installing. Students carry their completed poppies to the lawn and position them according to the grid, contributing to a shared community task.

This sequence connects the preparation of individual materials to the completion of a meaningful community project.

From a Montessori perspective, the work supports independence, concentration, coordination, order, and purposeful movement. The installation provides a visible result of collaborative effort while allowing each child to contribute through developmentally appropriate participation.

Canadian history and peace education

The field of red poppies connects students to the history of the First World War and the significance of the poppy as a Canadian symbol of remembrance.

Inspired by Lieutenant-Colonel John McCrae’s poem In Flanders Fields, the installation recalls the poppies associated with the battlefields and military cemeteries of Belgium and northern France.

The ordered arrangement evokes the rows of military graves, encouraging students to consider the human consequences of war and the importance of remembering those who lost their lives.

For younger children, the educational focus remains on kindness, gratitude, peace, and respectful participation. Older students can examine the historical origins of the poppy, the significance of the First World War, and the role of public memorials in preserving collective memory.

The installation should be understood not as a celebration of war, but as an acknowledgement of sacrifice and a commitment to peace.

Planning consideration

If every student prepares ten poppies, the school will require 100 participating students to reach the target of 1,000. If enrolment differs, additional poppies can be prepared through collaborative classroom work or family contributions.

For the installation itself, I recommend grouping the completed poppies into bundles of ten before placing them on the lawn. This would create a particularly valuable Montessori mathematical experience: children could physically observe how ten units make a ten, ten tens make one hundred, and ten hundreds make one thousand.

Final curriculum statement

A Field of Remembrance demonstrates how a school-wide installation can unite mathematical understanding, artistic expression, purposeful work, and historical remembrance.

By constructing 1,000 poppies and arranging them in carefully measured rows, students transform an abstract number into a visible, physical experience. Each child contributes to the collective field, discovering that individual effort becomes part of something greater.

The completed installation stands as both a concrete representation of 1,000 and a shared expression of gratitude, remembrance, and hope for peace.
